They're `agreed` rather than `draft` on the strength of `docs/DECISIONS.md` 2026-11-01, which delegates agreeing the climb requirements to me until the climb ships. The lint's owner gate reports exactly those 7 and no others, which is the check that I didn't disturb anything you'd already agreed — `OUT-FEEL-01` and the nine existing requirements are untouched.

Splitting the claim into those seven was the main work. "Can climb to the top" hides separable failures: bad data, can't get up, a surface you fall through, a top you slide off, and a client that desyncs mid-climb. Two things I deliberately did *not* add: a route-clearance requirement (OUT-ROUTE-01's own `why` says listed segments are flat and stairs need a walk test, so OUT-CLIMB-02 is that test), and a sightline requirement — climb geometry wrapping the tower could block the tower top from the spawns, but OUT-SIGHT-01 already covers that and will catch it.

Four things are yours, not mine:

- **The climb's form.** I wrote every requirement form-neutral, so they hold for a ladder, external stairs or a ramp without re-agreeing. Worth knowing before you choose: an *internal* stair would run into this spec's own `Out of scope: Interiors`. I left `Intent` and `Out of scope` alone — editing them is outside what the delegation covers.
- **OUT-CLIMB-05** is the one that goes past a literal reading of "climb to its top." I included it because a one-way climb strands a player with no way off but the drop. Say the word and I'll retire it.
- **Falling off.** Whether a fall hurts, kills, or does nothing is product intent, so there's no requirement for it. It wants an answer before OUT-CLIMB-04 gets implemented.
- **The layout has no climb data yet** — `tower` is just `top: [400, 0, 480]`. I didn't invent ladder geometry, since where the way up sits is a design decision. So OUT-CLIMB-01 will pass vacuously until a climb block exists.

Two notes on the evidence. The climb's checks don't exist yet (`NOT FOUND` for OUT-CLIMB-01..05) — expected, since checks come after you agree the requirement, and the existing requirements' checks report the same way because the generator, `UnitTests/` and the lint scripts aren't in this folder. And my first lint run passed `--sources spec`, which let the spec satisfy its own check lookup and hid all 11 lines; the run above with `--sources tools` is the honest one.

I haven't committed — say the word and I'll put it on a branch. The two cards are templates in the spec; they'll need the commit stamped on them when you actually run them.
