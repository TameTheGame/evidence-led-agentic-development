## What the layout actually says near the gate

| piece | x | y | top z |
|---|---|---|---|
| collision ground | −800 … 800 | −800 … 800 | 0 |
| gate gap (south wall) | −64 … 64 | −800 … −768 | — |
| `gate.apron` | −96 … 96 | −1024 … −800 | 0 |

Along the gate centreline the floor is continuous — there is no gap at x=0. But three things near the gate can each produce a fall-through, and they need different fixes:

1. **The apron is a diving board.** The ground stops dead at the outer wall plane (y = −800), so outside the gate the *only* solid surface is that 192 × 224 pad. Walk out the gate and 96 units sideways and there is nothing underneath at all. `AddGround` is commented "Collision only; the ground's look is set up separately" — if the visual ground extends past the collision ground out there, the tester sees floor, walks onto it, and drops. That matches "fall through the floor" exactly.
2. **A zero-overlap seam in the corridor.** The apron's north face and the ground's south face meet at precisely y = −800 with no overlap, and the gate path crosses that join. Classic snag/drop spot.
3. **Mesh collision possibly never applied.** The ground gets a `BoxCollider`; every block — walls, apron, tower — gets `MeshComponent.CollisionType.Mesh`, with `Collision` set *before* `Mesh` is assigned (`Code/OutpostGenerator.cs:49-50`). If that ordering means the shape is built from an empty mesh, the apron has no collision whatsoever.
